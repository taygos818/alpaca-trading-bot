Meet Taygos818 Options Bot: an autonomous, multi-agent system built for the Alpaca AI Trading Agents Hackathon.

The goal was not to automate a fixed watchlist. The bot searches a dynamic universe of U.S. equities, finds unusual market activity, checks whether each symbol supports options, and freezes the supporting market and catalyst evidence before any artificial intelligence sees it.

Three independent agents then examine the same evidence. The technical agent evaluates completed price bars, VWAP, opening-range behavior, trend, and volume. The catalyst agent decides whether news or unusual activity could persist through the session. The macro agent looks for supplied broad-market evidence and stays neutral when none exists. Every agent must return structured data, cite the exact evidence it used, and may abstain when the case is weak.

Artificial intelligence does not control the brokerage account. It cannot size a position, change a risk limit, invent a contract, or send an order. Deterministic code converts an agreed direction into a call or put debit spread, validates expiration, delta, liquidity, bid-ask width, reward versus risk, and maximum possible loss. An adversarial reviewer can object, and the portfolio risk engine has final veto authority.

Only a signed authorization reaches the pinned Alpaca command-line gateway. The gateway is permanently restricted to the paper endpoint. It previews and submits structured limit orders, prevents duplicates with deterministic client order identifiers, and reconciles new orders, partial fills, cancellations, positions, and restarts.

This dashboard is the bot's evidence trail. The heartbeat confirms the engine is running. The counters summarize recent traces, proposals, risk rejections, orders, and positions. Every opportunity is searchable and filterable. Selecting one reconstructs its ranking, independent-agent findings, chosen structure, deterministic risk outcome, paper-order lifecycle, position state, exit status, and evidence provenance.

The expandable activity log shows real lifecycle events, including submitted exits, pending orders, and confirmed fills. It is read-only and receives no broker credentials, so observability cannot become an execution backdoor.

Across the competition snapshot, the system recorded twenty-five thousand one hundred thirty-six unique decision traces, made eight thousand six hundred seventy-seven Featherless requests, submitted sixty entries and thirty exits, and recorded one hundred twenty-two option-leg fills. Alpaca reported no rejected orders.

The result was not profitable. The account moved from one hundred thousand dollars to eighty-six thousand three hundred twenty-one dollars and seventy-nine cents, a loss of thirteen point six eight percent. The aggressive contest profile exposed weaknesses in catalyst quality, retries, option-spread execution, and position sizing. A forced-flatten defect also left nine spreads open after unfilled exit limits reached the closing bell.

That honesty is part of the system. Taygos818 Options Bot does not merely show what it traded. It preserves why it acted, why it abstained, what risk approved, how Alpaca responded, and where the implementation failed. It is autonomous trading with a complete, inspectable record.
